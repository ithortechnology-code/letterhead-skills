# India letterhead notes (general guidance, NOT legal advice)

Tell the user to confirm anything below with their CA / Company Secretary. Rules change and differ by entity type.

## What a letterhead normally carries
- **Company (Pvt / Public Ltd):** the Companies Act, 2013 (s.12) expects name, registered office address and **CIN**
  on business letters, bill heads and letter papers; phone, email and website too where they exist.
- **LLP:** name (ending in "LLP"), registered office address and **LLPIN** are expected on business letters.
  The official identifier is called **LLPIN** (format like `AAA-1234`), not "LLPNO".
- **Proprietorship / Partnership firm:** no registration number is mandated on letters, but GSTIN, Udyam, PAN are customary.
- **GSTIN:** mandatory on tax invoices; on ordinary letters it is customary, not required.

## Seals and signatures
- A **common/official seal is not legally required** for companies (optional since 2015) and the LLP Act does not
  require one for LLPs. It is conventional, and banks / authorities often still ask for it. Make it optional per letter.
- Letters are signed by an authorised signatory (director / designated partner / person authorised by board resolution
  or LLP agreement). Name and designation under the signature is good practice.
- A **Digital Signature Certificate (DSC)** is for MCA/e-filing and similar, not for ordinary letters.

## When a letterhead is not enough
- Affidavits, indemnities, some undertakings and declarations need **stamp paper** (state-specific duty) and often
  **notarisation**. A printed letterhead does not replace that.
- Bank / government submissions may need a board resolution or authority letter attached.

## Reference numbering
- Indian practice is usually `PREFIX/FY/NNN`, e.g. `ACME/2026-27/001`, financial year April to March. Config `refScheme`: `fy` (default), `calendar`, `none`.
